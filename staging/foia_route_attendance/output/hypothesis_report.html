Empirical Hypothesis Testing: Route Attendance vs. 311 Misses

Quantitative validation of investigative hypotheses using DPW FOIA records, 311 requests, and meteorological observations
Analytic Time Window April 4, 2026 to September 30, 2026 (180 Days)
Route Attendance Dataset 7,183 Route-Days across 123 Service Dates (FOIA R014873-093026)
311 Overlap Dataset 20,401 311 Records (DC Open Data Layer 21, with 5d lag buffer)
Meteorological Dataset 183 Daily Observations (Washington, DC Centroid, Open-Meteo Archive)

Hypothesis 1: The Call-Out / Absenteeism Mechanism

Premise: DPW leadership asserts that missed pickups are driven by crew call-outs. When workers call out, route completion degrades or segments are missed. Because residents report missed service in the evening or on following days, we analyze same-day (Lag 0), next-day (Lag +1), and cumulative 3-day windows.

Premise: The site charts show Monday routes are consistently the least reliable and Thursday routes the most reliable. We test whether this disparity is driven by higher Monday absenteeism ("Garfield effect") or if attendance is uniform across weekdays.

This refutes the pure "Garfield" absenteeism hypothesis: Monday unreliability is driven primarily by post-weekend waste accumulation and route scheduling, not higher worker call-outs.

Hypothesis 4: Weather Seasonality & Environmental Disruption (Day-of-Week Controlled)

Overview: DPW leadership repeatedly cited adverse weather conditions—particularly record summer heat—as a primary driver of service disruptions. However, as established in Hypothesis 3, Day-of-Week is a massive confounding variable (Monday baseline leave is 16.0% vs. Thursday 11.0%; Monday 3-day complaints average 2.80 vs. Thursday 1.94). For example, severe heat (>= 95F) happened only on Tuesdays, Wednesdays, Thursdays, and Fridays—never on a Monday—artificially deflating unadjusted attendance figures. Below, we purge day-of-week calendar bias using both Day-of-Week Normalization (Expected vs. Observed Deviations) and Econometric Fixed-Effects OLS Regression across: 4a (Ambient Temperature), 4b (Precipitation & Rainfall), and 4c (Compound Interaction of Heat and Rain).
