Key Operational Patterns Identified

Core logistical and systemic failure modes mapped to specific geographic areas and neighborhood corridors.
Pattern 1 • Concentration Skew

Extreme Pareto Distribution Across Truck Routes

Missed collection complaints are heavily concentrated in a small minority of truck routes. The top 10 trash routes account for 23.1% of all missed trash tickets District-wide (1,432 of 6,197 requests). Similarly, the top 10 recycling routes drive 21.9% of all recycling failures. While the median trash route generated 51 requests over 180 days, high-failure corridors such as Route OR304 (Tenleytown / Spring Valley / Hawthorne • 220 reqs) and Route IC203 (Dupont Circle / Connecticut Ave / Cardozo • 198 reqs) experienced more than 4x the median volume.

Pattern 2 • Schedule Bottlenecks

Mid-Week Wednesday Trash Peak & Early-Week Recycling Surge

Evaluating tickets by scheduled pickup day reveals distinct weekly operational bottlenecks. For trash collection, Wednesday routes lead the District with 1,369 service requests (982 unique properties), driven by large Upper Northeast and Upper Northwest runs such as Route OR504 in Brookland/Brentwood (122 reqs). For recycling, early-week operations on Monday and Tuesday account for 48.7% of all missed recycling complaints (1,245 of 2,554 requests), led by Route R323_1 in Chevy Chase/Barnaby Woods (83 reqs) and Route R209_2 in Dupont Circle (71 reqs).

Pattern 3 • Failure Typology

Widespread Route Skips vs. Chronic Resolution Failures

Address-level deduplication identifies two fundamentally different failure mechanics:

Widespread Route Bypasses: Routes such as OR706 (Ward 7 • Burrville, Deanwood, NE Boundary) and OR708 (Ward 7 • River Terrace, Twining, Dupont Park) exhibit high unique address counts (96 to 98 properties) but relatively low repeat rates (18.4% to 21.9%). Collection crews bypassed entire blockfaces or alley corridors on pickup day, but follow-up servicing generally resolved the issue without repeat tickets on subsequent weeks.

Chronic Problem Resolution Failures: In contrast, corridors like Recycling Route R209_2 (Ward 2 • Dupont Circle, Connecticut Ave, K St) and Trash Route OR309 (Ward 3 • Friendship Heights, AU Park, Tenleytown) suffer from persistent recurrence. On R209_2, 50.0% of all complaining properties (14 of 28 addresses) submitted tickets across 2 to 5 distinct collection cycles, averaging 2.54 requests per complaining property. This demonstrates that closing 311 tickets failed to fix the underlying physical obstacles (narrow alleys, parked cars, or construction).

Pattern 4 • Service Area Paradox

Twice-Weekly Inner City Service Shows Stubborn Recurrence

DPW divides trash collection into two operational models: Outer Ring (once-weekly residential collection) and Inner City (twice-weekly collection in dense historic rowhouse and commercial corridors).

Despite receiving twice the collection frequency, Inner City routes average 61.5 requests per route (comparable to 59.7 reqs/route in the Outer Ring). Crucially, Route IC203 (Ward 2/1 • Dupont Circle, Connecticut Ave, Cardozo) ranks #2 across all 103 trash routes in the city with 198 requests and a 32.0% multi-day repeat rate. Tight historic alleyways, parked vehicle obstructions, and overflow container crowding frequently prevent trucks from completing alleys, leading to chronic repeat failures despite two weekly passes.

Data Sources & Methodology: 311 service request records for Missed Trash (S0441) and Missed Recycling (S0321) were retrieved from the Open Data DC GIS FeatureServer spanning March 24, 2026 through September 20, 2026. Service requests were spatially joined to DPW Collection Route polygon boundaries (140 Trash route features and 173 Recycling route features). Geographic areas, neighborhoods, and ANCs were calculated using spatial intersection with official DC GIS 2023 Single Member District and Neighborhood Cluster boundaries. Properties were deduplicated using unique Master Address Repository (MAR) IDs and street address strings. Repeat addresses were calculated by identifying properties with service requests submitted across two or more distinct collection calendar dates. All data is public domain.
